Local discovery has split into two high-value surfaces: Google's **three-result local Map Pack** and the **AI-generated answer layer** that increasingly appears above or around conventional search results. Each compresses a large field of competitors into a very short list.

That compression changes the economics of visibility. The question is no longer simply whether a business ranks somewhere on page one. The practical questions are:

- Does the business appear in the three local results a customer sees before choosing “More places”?
- Is the business—or a trusted source that validates it—present in the AI-generated answer?
- Does that visibility produce calls, directions, forms, appointments, and booked revenue?

This report synthesizes current 2025–2026 research to explain what those positions may be worth, what the available studies actually prove, and which improvements support both forms of discovery.

> **Important interpretation note:** click-through-rate studies describe sampled search behavior, not a guaranteed return for every business. Category, geography, device, SERP layout, brand recognition, seasonality, offer quality, and response speed can all materially change the result.

## The two front doors to local demand

The Map Pack and AI Overviews are related, but they are not interchangeable.

### Google Map Pack

- Displays a map and a small set of local businesses for geographically relevant searches.
- Uses a local ranking system that Google describes through **relevance, distance, and prominence**.
- Draws heavily on the Google Business Profile and corroborating information across the web.
- Creates direct paths to calls, websites, directions, and visits.

Google states that complete and accurate information makes a business more likely to appear in relevant local results. Its public guidance also identifies reviews, positive ratings, and links as signals that can contribute to prominence. For the operating details, see our companion report, [How the Google Local Pack Really Works](/reports/how-the-google-local-pack-really-works/).

### AI Overviews and AI-assisted discovery

- Generate a synthesized response rather than a conventional ranked list.
- Cite a limited group of web pages and sources that support the answer.
- Can resolve an informational question before the searcher visits another site.
- Create a second visibility challenge: a business may rank conventionally without being cited or recommended by an AI system.

The strategic consequence is simple: **local rank and AI visibility must be measured separately**. Strong entity data and public corroboration help both, but success in one surface does not automatically produce success in the other.

## Why the top three Map Pack positions matter

The Map Pack displays three businesses before a user expands the result. That interface makes fourth place more than a one-position decline: it can move a business behind an additional interaction.

A widely cited First Page Sage click-through-rate benchmark reports the following distribution for local-pack placements:

| Local result | Reported average CTR |
| --- | ---: |
| Map Pack position 1 | 17.6% |
| Map Pack position 2 | 15.4% |
| Map Pack position 3 | 15.1% |
| Top paid search result | 2.1% |

On that benchmark, the first three local results collectively account for **48.1 clicks per 100 search impressions**. The first Map Pack position's 17.6% reported CTR is about **8.4 times** the study's 2.1% benchmark for the top paid result.

Those numbers should be used as a scenario input—not copied into a revenue forecast as fact. The useful economic model is:

> **Estimated opportunity = relevant search impressions × observed CTR × qualified-action rate × close rate × customer value**

For example, a market with 2,000 relevant monthly impressions and an observed 15% CTR would produce an estimated 300 visits or profile actions before conversion and sales assumptions are applied. A credible forecast replaces every generic benchmark with data from the business's Google Business Profile performance, Search Console, analytics, call tracking, CRM, and booked-work records.

## Local intent shortens the path from query to revenue

Local search is economically attractive because many queries include a location, a service need, and near-term intent. A person searching for an emergency plumber, nearby clinic, contractor, or material supplier is often closer to action than someone reading a general educational article.

Older Google consumer research is still frequently cited for the finding that **76% of people who conducted a local search on a smartphone visited a related business within a day**, while **28% of local searches resulted in a purchase**. These figures are useful directional context, but they should not be treated as a current universal conversion rate.

The durable takeaway is the behavioral sequence:

1. A local need creates a geographically constrained search.
2. The search result creates a small initial shortlist.
3. Profile accuracy, reviews, availability, photos, and website clarity reduce uncertainty.
4. A call, form, direction request, or booking creates the measurable business opportunity.
5. Response speed and sales handling determine how much of that opportunity becomes revenue.

Visibility therefore cannot be evaluated in isolation. A high Map Pack position attached to an incomplete profile, weak offer, slow website, or unanswered inquiry can still waste demand. The operating system after the click matters, which is why [response speed belongs in the local-visibility model](/reports/why-speed-wins/).

## AI Overviews changed click economics

AI Overviews moved quickly from an experiment to a prominent search-result feature. Their prevalence varies substantially by query set, which explains why studies can report different headline percentages without necessarily contradicting one another.

Semrush's analysis of more than 10 million keywords found AI Overview prevalence rising from **6.49% in January 2025** to nearly **25% in July 2025**, then declining to **15.69% in November 2025**. That is a mixed-query dataset, not a local-only sample.

Local-query research shows a different pattern. Whitespark's May 2025 study found AI Overviews in approximately **68% of the local-business searches it tested**. The feature appeared most often for informational and hybrid queries—the questions people ask while deciding what to buy, such as cost, comparison, and “best option” searches.

The click effect is material. Ahrefs compared 300,000 informational keywords and reported that, in December 2025, the presence of an AI Overview correlated with a **58% lower average click-through rate for the top-ranking page**. The methodology compares groups of queries and supports a directional conclusion; it does not prove that every individual keyword will lose exactly 58% of its clicks.

For a local business, this creates two simultaneous jobs:

- Win the action-oriented surface where customers call, request directions, or visit a site.
- Become an extractable, corroborated source for the questions customers ask before taking that action.

## AI recommendations are more selective than local results

SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index analyzed more than **350,000 locations across 2,751 multi-location brands**. SOCi reported that ChatGPT recommended **1.2% of brand locations**, compared with a **35.9% average appearance rate in Google's local three-pack** within its dataset.

That does not mean every local business has a 1.2% chance of appearing in an AI answer. The study focuses on multi-location brands and measures a particular set of prompts and platforms. It does show how much narrower a recommendation interface can be than a ranked local-results interface.

SOCi also reported only **45% overlap** between the retail brands most visible in traditional local search and those most frequently recommended by AI platforms. The finding reinforces a practical measurement rule: **do not use Google rank as a proxy for AI visibility**.

AI systems assemble answers from multiple sources, which can include:

- the business's own service and location pages
- Google Business Profile and map data
- review and directory platforms
- editorial publishers and community discussions
- structured facts that are consistent across those sources

That source mix makes [entity strength](/reports/entity-strength-and-ai-search-visibility-for-local-service-businesses/) more useful than a website-only view of authority. A system is more likely to trust and reproduce a business fact when multiple credible sources describe the same entity consistently.

## The economic reality: model the whole funnel

Visibility has economic value only when it creates incremental qualified demand. The cleanest way to value a Map Pack or AI visibility improvement is to model each stage explicitly.

| Funnel stage | Measure with | Question to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search demand | Search Console, keyword tracking, market research | How many relevant opportunities exist? |
| Local visibility | Map-grid tracking, GBP performance | Where and how often does the business appear? |
| AI visibility | Prompt tracking and cited-source review | Is the brand mentioned, recommended, or cited? |
| Action rate | Calls, forms, bookings, direction requests | What percentage becomes a real inquiry? |
| Qualification | CRM and call review | How many inquiries match the desired work? |
| Close rate | CRM and sales records | How many qualified opportunities become customers? |
| Customer value | Finance and retention data | What is the gross profit or lifetime value? |

This model prevents two common mistakes. The first is valuing rankings without measuring actions. The second is treating every click or AI mention as equally valuable. A lower-volume query tied to a high-margin service may be worth more than a broad query that produces curiosity but no qualified demand.

## Six trends shaping local visibility

### 1. Local rank and AI visibility are separate scoreboards

The signals overlap, but the outputs do not. Track Map Pack coverage, conventional organic performance, AI mentions, AI citations, and leads as distinct measures.

### 2. Zero-click behavior raises the value of on-result persuasion

Customers can evaluate ratings, hours, photos, service details, and AI-generated summaries without visiting a website. The public profile and the facts cited by other sources now do more of the selling.

### 3. The Google Business Profile is core entity infrastructure

An accurate profile supports map visibility and supplies facts that can appear throughout Google's ecosystem. It should be managed as a living operational asset, not a one-time listing task.

### 4. Reviews influence discovery and conversion

Google publicly identifies review count and positive ratings as factors that can contribute to local prominence. Reviews also help customers reduce uncertainty. Volume alone is not the goal; authenticity, recency, detail, response quality, and policy compliance matter.

### 5. Extractable content is becoming table stakes

Clear headings, concise answers, consistent entity facts, internal links, and valid structured data make content easier for search and AI systems to interpret. Schema does not guarantee a citation, but it reduces ambiguity.

### 6. Measurement must follow the customer, not the ranking tool

Rank is an intermediate signal. The durable scorecard connects visibility to qualified conversations, booked work, gross profit, and customer value.

## The local visibility playbook

### 1. Complete and govern the Google Business Profile

Use the most accurate primary category, complete every relevant field, maintain regular and special hours, add current photos, and verify that service and contact information match the real business.

### 2. Enforce entity consistency

Audit the business name, address or service area, phone, hours, categories, and URLs across Google, Apple Maps, major review platforms, industry directories, and the website. Consistency helps customers and machines resolve the business as one entity.

### 3. Build an ethical review operating system

Ask real customers for honest feedback, make the process easy, respond thoughtfully, and monitor review patterns. Never buy, gate, or incentivize reviews in ways that violate platform rules.

### 4. Publish useful service and location evidence

Create pages that explain the work, market, process, constraints, proof, and next step. Avoid interchangeable city pages. Local relevance should come from real market knowledge and useful customer information.

### 5. Answer hybrid-intent questions clearly

Cost, timing, comparison, eligibility, and “best fit” questions frequently appear during purchase evaluation. Answer them in self-contained passages with direct headings, supporting evidence, and clear caveats.

### 6. Implement structured data that matches the visible page

Use valid `LocalBusiness`, `Service`, `Article`, `BreadcrumbList`, and other appropriate Schema.org types. Structured data should describe visible, truthful content; it should not introduce claims the visitor cannot see.

### 7. Earn third-party corroboration

Maintain accurate profiles on trusted platforms, earn legitimate local and industry mentions, and publish research or proof that others have a reason to reference.

### 8. Measure share of model and share of market

Track representative prompts across Google AI features, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity alongside local map-grid coverage and conventional search. Record both whether the brand appears and which source is cited.

## Methodology and limits

This report is a synthesis of third-party datasets published across different dates, markets, devices, query classes, and methodologies. The figures should not be combined as though they came from a single controlled study.

Use the research to form hypotheses and scenario ranges. Validate investment decisions against first-party performance data from the specific business and market. Recheck time-sensitive benchmarks before using them in forecasts or client commitments.

Marketing spend multiplies nothing if the front desk drops the call. For a law firm the order of work is therefore intake first, then visibility: make sure every inquiry that arrives is answered quickly, then make sure more of the right inquiries arrive, including the ones that now begin inside an AI assistant.

The figures below describe United States legal marketing, not Oregon queries specifically. We have not measured Oregon legal search ourselves, and we say so where it matters.

## Intake is where legal marketing leaks

Legal clients decide fast and by phone. Research on 2026 call-tracking and conversion data reports:

| Measure | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Prospective clients who contact a firm within 24 hours of searching | 76% |
| Legal conversions that happen by telephone | 56.3% |
| Legal consumers who hire the first attorney they speak with | over 60% |
| Inbound calls missed during business hours | up to 35% |
| Generated leads not captured overall | 60% |
| Conversion lift for a response within five minutes, against after an hour | 400% |
| Conversion increase for personal injury firms using AI intake that responds in under 30 seconds | 40% |

In a Smith.ai analysis of 3.2 million answered calls across 4,210 U.S. law firms (October 2026), personal injury firms connected 20.6% of qualified callers overall. Firms whose receptionists or AI agents could book directly on a calendar connected 69.3%, against 15.3% for firms without scheduling.

The practical reading: before adding more traffic, measure what happens to the traffic you already pay for.

## Search now includes the answer, not just the link

Research on 2026 legal search reports that AI Overviews appear on more than 77% of legal queries, up from about 12% of legal discovery queries involving AI citations in 2024, the highest trigger rate of any industry it compared. People also ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude directly for a firm by name and practice area.

Two consequences matter for a firm's website:

- Ranking and citation have separated. A page can rank first in the traditional results and be left out of the AI Overview, while an entity-optimized, well-structured page can be cited without ranking on the first page.
- AI referrals convert well. The same Ruler Analytics study of more than 110 million sessions reports 8.4% conversion for AI referral traffic, behind only referral traffic in the legal sector (8.8%), and well above organic search at 3.0% to 4.3%.

## What GEO for a law firm involves

Generative engine optimization, GEO, is the work of making a firm easy for AI assistants to find, understand and cite. For a law firm the sequence is:

1. **Baseline.** A two-to-three-week audit of what ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews say about the firm today.
2. **Entity authority.** Consistent profiles in the places assistants read: Google Business Profile, state bar directories, Martindale-Hubbell, Avvo, Justia and court records where they apply.
3. **Answer pages.** Practice-area pages rewritten as jurisdiction-specific questions and answers with FAQPage schema that matches the visible text.
4. **Name, address and phone consistency.** For multi-office firms, a two-to-four-week cleanup so conflicting entity data does not suppress citations.
5. **Attorney review without bottlenecks.** A light workflow that captures the billing attorney's real expertise and signs off every claim.
6. **Tracking.** Monthly counts of how often assistants name the firm, alongside calls and booked consultations.

## Compliance is part of the work

Under ABA Model Rule 7.1, a lawyer may not make false or misleading communications about their services, and that includes website copy, attorney biographies, blog articles and chatbot answers. ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) puts the duty to verify AI-generated claims on the attorney. Some states, Florida among them, add rules for chatbots and AI voices, such as screening for existing representation and disclosing that an intake assistant is not a lawyer.

So we do not promise case outcomes or rankings, and nothing about a firm's cases, results or statutes is published without the attorney's sign-off. Advertising rules differ by state; the final review stays with the attorney and the state bar's rules.

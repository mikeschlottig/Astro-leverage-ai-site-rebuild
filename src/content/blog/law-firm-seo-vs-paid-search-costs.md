> **TL;DR**
>
> - Attorneys and legal services average $131.63 per lead in paid search, nearly double the all-industry average of $66.69.
> - Personal injury keywords average over $150 per click in competitive metros and medical malpractice often exceeds $200. Google Local Services Ads average $232 per lead.
> - Legal SEO benchmarks reach break-even around month 14 and return 526% over three years, but those figures come from firms investing $120,000 to $150,000 a year.
> - A click is a rental. A ranking, and the entity data behind it, is an asset you keep.

Paid search is a landlord relationship. You pay for the click, and when the budget stops, the traffic stops. Organic search and AI citation work more like ownership: the page, the listings and the structured data stay yours and keep earning.

That framing is the same one we apply to website ownership. This article gives the actual auction math.

## What a click actually costs

Benchmarks across Google and Microsoft Ads show the "Attorneys and Legal Services" category carrying an average cost per lead of $131.63, against $66.69 across all industries.

Cost per click varies sharply by practice area:

| Practice area | Reported cost |
| --- | --- |
| Family law | $7.69 average cost per click |
| Personal injury (competitive metros) | over $150 per click |
| Medical malpractice | routinely over $200 per click |
| Google Local Services Ads | $232 average per lead |

Family law, a lower-value volume practice, is moderate. Contingency-fee litigation auctions are not. A personal injury firm competing in a hot metro can pay more for one click than many businesses pay for a lead.

## Why small firms feel it worst

A large plaintiff firm can spread a high cost per lead across a large intake operation and a brand that lifts every channel. A solo or small firm cannot. The same research reports that small practices allocate roughly $5,000 to $50,000 a year across all marketing channels, and that 74% of law firms report wasting capital on underperforming campaigns. When the auction price rises, a small firm hits its budget cap first and is left with nothing built at the end.

## The compounding case, and its limits

Against paid costs, organic search and AI answer optimization offer better compounding economics for firms that can wait through the ramp-up. Performance benchmarks report that legal SEO typically reaches break-even at month 14, drives an average 21% annual increase in organic traffic, and returns 526% over three years.

Read the fine print. Those benchmarks come from firms investing an average of $120,000 to $150,000 a year in SEO. A solo firm on a $1,500 to $4,000 monthly retainer (the range the research reports for low-competition markets, rising to $3,000 to $6,000 for moderate ones) will not follow the same curve. Use the pattern, not the exact percentages: the cost is front-loaded and the return compounds.

## Intake changes the arithmetic

A cost per lead is only as good as what happens to the lead. If a firm misses up to 35% of calls and fails to capture 60% of its leads, a $131.63 lead is worth less than it looks. Fixing response speed and scheduling lowers the effective cost of every paid and organic lead you already buy. Start with [why firms lose 60% of leads at intake](/blog/law-firm-lead-intake-leak/).

## The hybrid answer

The research does not argue for abandoning paid search. Leading firms build an organic GEO foundation and align it with conversion-focused paid search and Local Services Ads for immediate demand. Paid fills the gap while organic compounds. The foundation is [AI Overviews and GEO](/blog/ai-overviews-legal-search-geo-visibility/) work; see [GEO for Law Firms](/blog/geo-for-law-firms-playbook/) for the steps.

## When paid search still makes sense

- You need cases this month and cannot wait fourteen months.
- You have an intake process that converts, so a paid lead is not wasted.
- You are testing a new practice area before investing in content.
- Your ad accounts and landing pages are owned by the firm, not the vendor.

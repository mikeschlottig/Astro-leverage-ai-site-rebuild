> **TL;DR**
>
> - AI models weight citations in tiers. Tier 1 is earned media, court records and bar registries. Tier 2 is authoritative legal directories: Martindale-Hubbell, Avvo, FindLaw, Justia, Super Lawyers and Best Lawyers. Tier 3 is general listings.
> - Keep 100% consistent name, address, phone and website (NAP+W), complete practice-area taxonomies, peer endorsements and steady reviews across Avvo, Justia, FindLaw, Martindale-Hubbell and Google Business Profile.
> - Leaving a directory as a website vendor is a different decision from removing the listing.

Everyone stopped investing in legal directories because "nobody uses them." For many firms that matched the evidence: directory leads were thin and the vendor relationship was poor. The mistake is to conclude the listings are worthless.

## Why everyone quit directories

Directory vendors sold templated websites bundled with listing placement and monthly SEO. Firms bought them as one package. As the packages aged and as ownership of the main directories consolidated (we cover that in [FindLaw After the Buyout](/blog/findlaw-website-ownership-law-firms/)), many firms decided the whole category was a legacy expense. For client acquisition directly, that is a fair read.

## What changed: the recommendation layer

When someone asks an assistant who a good lawyer near them is, the answer is built from sources the assistant trusts. Research on 2026 legal citation behavior reports that AI models weight external citations in a strict hierarchy:

| Tier | Signal | What it consists of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Highest | Earned third-party media, court records and government or bar registries that cannot be self-published |
| 2 | Strong | Authoritative legal directories: Martindale-Hubbell, Avvo, FindLaw, Justia, Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers |
| 3 | Baseline | General business listings |

Tier-2 directories sit high because they are structured, verified and heavily indexed as trusted legal-domain sources. The paradox is that a directory's usefulness as a place clients browse is falling while its usefulness as evidence to a machine is rising.

## The five listings that still matter in 2026

The research names five places where consistency and completeness now count as a foundational requirement for AI discoverability:

1. Avvo
2. Justia
3. FindLaw
4. Martindale-Hubbell
5. Google Business Profile

Bar registries and court records belong in Tier 1 and need the same attention, because they are the hardest signals to fake.

## NAP+W: the consistency tax

NAP+W means name, address, phone and website URL. Hold it identical across every listing, down to suite abbreviations and the way the firm name is written. For a firm with several offices or an attorney who changed firms, drift accumulates silently. A conflicting listing is a data point an engine has to resolve, and engines tend to resolve conflict by trusting less. A cleanup sprint of two to four weeks for a multi-office firm is typical in the research.

Add the other fields the directories support: an exhaustive practice-area taxonomy, peer endorsements and a current attorney bio.

## Review velocity: a signal AI systems read

Review velocity, the pace at which fresh client reviews arrive, is part of how Google's Local Services Ads rank and part of the foundation the research lists for AI discoverability. A steady stream of recent, genuine reviews says the practice is active. Ask every satisfied client, make it easy, and follow your state's rules on soliciting and responding to reviews.

## The vendor-versus-listing decision framework

Treat the two decisions separately:

| Decision | Question | Keep or leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The vendor relationship | Do we own our site, content and accounts, and does the service justify the price? | Leave if not; see the exit checklist |
| The listing | Is it accurate, complete and consistent with every other listing? | Keep and maintain |

You can end a vendor contract and keep, claim and correct the free or paid listing. Do the migration in that order: secure ownership, then cut over, then clean up the listings so the new site and the directories agree.

## What happens next

The research forecasts, as an inference for 2028 to 2031, that directory owners may shift from selling templated sites toward licensing verified attorney data to AI providers and charging firms for "verified AI entity placement." That is a forecast, not a measurement. It is a reason to own your own accurate profile data now.

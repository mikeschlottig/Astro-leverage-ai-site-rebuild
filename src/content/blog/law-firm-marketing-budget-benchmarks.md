> **TL;DR**
>
> - Law firms allocate an average of 2% to 10% of gross revenue to marketing, and about 45% of the digital budget goes to SEO.
> - Solo and small practices (1 to 9 attorneys) spend roughly $5,000 to $50,000 a year; mid-sized regional firms $50,000 to $500,000.
> - Monthly SEO and AI-search retainers run $1,500 to $6,000 for small firms and $5,000 to $15,000 for mid-sized firms.
> - Allocation is the problem more than size: 74% of firms report wasting money on underperforming campaigns, and retainers under $2,000 rarely fund the architecture and verification needed.

There is no single "right" marketing budget for a law firm, but there are benchmarks. This post gives the 2026 ranges by firm size. They are national figures from industry research; Southern Oregon firms should treat them as a reference point, not a quote.

## The 2% to 10% rule

Documented benchmarks show law firms allocate an average of 2% to 10% of gross annual revenue to marketing. High-growth and consumer plaintiff practices routinely exceed 10%. On average, 45% of the total digital marketing budget goes to search engine optimization.

## Tier 1: solo and small practices

For firms of one to nine attorneys (under 25 staff, revenue under $2 million):

- **Total marketing budget:** $5,000 to $50,000 a year, 2% to 10% of gross revenue.
- **Monthly SEO and AI-search retainer:** $1,500 to $4,000 in low-competition markets, $3,000 to $6,000 in moderate ones.
- **Web design and CMS:** $2,000 to $15,000 upfront, or a software subscription from about $82.50 a month.
- **Objectives:** Google Business Profile, local map pack, a basic site and call tracking.

## Tier 2: mid-sized regional firms

For firms of 10 to 99 attorneys:

- **Total marketing budget:** $50,000 to $500,000 a year, 7% to 10% of gross revenue.
- **Monthly retainer:** $5,000 to $9,500 in moderate markets, $8,000 to $15,000 in competitive ones.
- **Web design and CMS:** $15,000 to $50,000 for a custom WordPress or Webflow build.
- **Objectives:** multi-location local SEO, content velocity, AI-search schema and CRM integration.

## Tier 3: large consumer and multi-location plaintiff firms

Firms with 20 to 500 or more attorneys spend from about $500,000 to tens of millions a year, often above 10% of revenue, with retainers starting around $15,000 a month and web builds of $50,000 to $200,000 or more. The research describes this tier as industrial-scale arbitrage, tied to mass media and call attribution.

## Tier 4: AmLaw 200 and enterprise

Corporate and enterprise firms compete on a different basis: no consumer "near me" queries, with web transformation projects reported at $99,000 to over $850,000. This is a different sport and the benchmarks above do not apply to it.

## The 45% allocation

If 45% of a digital budget goes to SEO, a firm spending $60,000 a year online would put about $27,000 into search. The remaining 55% covers the site, paid media, listings and tools. Treat this as a starting split to challenge, not a rule.

## Where budgets leak

Budget size is not the main problem. Allocation is:

- 74% of law firms report wasting capital on underperforming marketing campaigns.
- 97% lack personalized content on their websites.
- 56% of law firm domains lack basic technical trust signals such as a properly configured SSL certificate.
- Monthly SEO retainers under $2,000 rarely fund the technical architecture and editorial verification needed to rank ethically, which leaves small firms exposed to algorithmic penalties from low-quality output.
- Unedited AI text can create both ranking and ethics risk; see [AI Marketing Compliance for Law Firms](/blog/ai-marketing-compliance-law-firms/).

A practical caveat on that last retainer point: a bounded scope, such as profile cleanup, consistency and core schema, can still be worth doing below $2,000. The finding is that a retainer cannot fund broad content and architecture work at that price.

## A one-page budget template

1. Take last year's gross revenue and multiply by 2%, 5% and 10%. Choose a band.
2. Split the digital share: about 45% to SEO and AI search as a starting point.
3. Reserve budget for intake: scheduling and fast response often return more than extra traffic. See [why firms lose 60% of leads at intake](/blog/law-firm-lead-intake-leak/).
4. Decide paid versus owned. Read [The $150 Click](/blog/law-firm-seo-vs-paid-search-costs/).
5. Confirm you own your site and accounts; see [Who Owns Your Law Firm's Website?](/blog/findlaw-website-ownership-law-firms/).
6. Set one outcome metric: signed retainers per month and cost per signed retainer.

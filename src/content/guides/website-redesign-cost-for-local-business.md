A website redesign can be a visual refresh, a conversion repair, a content migration, a local-search rebuild, or a new operating system for leads. Those are not the same project, so they should not carry the same price.

## Current Leverage AI ranges

The current diagnostic-first structure is:

- **$500–$1,200:** diagnostic audit
- **$1,500–$4,000:** scoped system implementation
- **$400–$800 per month:** optional ongoing adaptation

Those ranges describe Leverage AI's present engagement structure, not a universal Oregon market average. A larger content inventory, ecommerce operation, custom application, complex migration, or multi-location program can require a materially larger budget.

## What changes redesign cost

### Strategy and diagnosis

A redesign is less expensive when the business already knows its audience, offers, priority services, markets, proof, and conversion path. If those decisions are unresolved, discovery is part of the project rather than an optional preface.

### Content condition

Reusing strong content is different from restructuring weak content, writing new service pages, consolidating duplicates, or building evidence for multiple locations.

### Migration risk

An established site may already carry rankings, backlinks, indexed URLs, forms, analytics, and integrations. Preserving that value requires an inventory, redirect map, metadata review, internal-link plan, and post-launch checks.

### Design and component scope

A focused service-business site built from a reusable system is different from dozens of one-off layouts. The cost should reflect the number of meaningful templates and content types, not just the raw page count.

### Local SEO and entity work

Service pages, location pages, Google Business Profile alignment, schema, citations, and internal links may belong in the redesign if the website is part of a local discovery system.

### Lead capture and integrations

Forms, call tracking, scheduling, CRM routing, missed-call recovery, email notifications, analytics, and consent requirements can change implementation scope substantially.

## A useful scope comparison

| Redesign type | Usually includes | Primary risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual refresh | Existing content in a cleaner theme | The original conversion and search problems remain |
| Conversion rebuild | Offer hierarchy, proof, calls to action, forms | Traffic exists but does not become qualified contact |
| Local-search rebuild | Services, locations, links, schema, profile alignment | Existing visibility is lost or local relevance stays thin |
| Platform migration | New framework or CMS, redirects, content transfer | Indexed value, forms, or integrations break at launch |
| Operating-system rebuild | Search, trust, capture, response, measurement | Scope becomes vague unless diagnosis comes first |

## Costs that should be visible before signing

Ask the provider to identify:

- one-time strategy and implementation fees
- content writing and migration responsibility
- hosting and domain ownership
- software and third-party subscription costs
- analytics, call tracking, and form-routing costs
- maintenance and support terms
- ownership of code, design files, content, and data
- what happens when the engagement ends

## Protect existing search value

Before launch, record the current URLs, titles, canonicals, important internal links, rankings, Search Console landing pages, conversions, and backlinks. Preserve useful URLs where possible and map necessary redirects deliberately.

A redesign that looks better but erases the site's existing discovery value is not a successful redesign.

## Start by pricing the constraint

If the current site mainly needs clearer service language and stronger calls to action, a focused implementation may be enough. If the architecture, content, local signals, forms, and measurement are all broken, a larger system rebuild may be justified.

The diagnostic audit exists to separate those situations before the business funds the wrong version of the project.
